Last updated: 30 October 2026

Status: Razorweld chart settings used by me, not formally tested. Everything else on this page is compiled research, not yet tried.

Applies to
#

My Razorweld Razorcut 45 on 230 V (20–45 A; on 120 V it’s only 20–30 A) with the X45 machine torch, the 51206 “20–50 amp” tip, and the machine shield, on a CrossFire PRO with IHS and THC (300 ipm max). Materials: mild steel, stainless, and aluminum, mostly 3/16 in and thinner. Doesn’t cover:

  • Hypertherm, Everlast, and Primeweld charts as settings. The T45m-format chart below is in, because FSFab reports Jasic supplied it for the Razorcut 45. Numbers from experienced members on other cutters (Everlast, PrimeWeld CUT60, Hypertherm) are rough guides only.
  • The “Razorcut 45 chart” in thread 10695, which a long-time forum member says is for the older IPT-style torch.
  • Any 120 V figures.

I couldn’t find an X45 machine-torch cut chart published by Langmuir (Langmuir pages checked in 2026; thread 11738). My starting chart is manufacturer data that came via the forum: Razorweld sent it to forum member MO-Hacker, who posted it on 2019-09-20: “Here is the cut chart that Razorweld sent me for the 45” (https://forum.langmuirsystems.com/t/847/29). PWCNC (t/847/45) and TinWhisperer (t/21266/8) reposted it. I’ve cut with these settings and they worked well, but they haven’t been formally tested on my machine yet. The test-cut program is the plan for that.

How sources are weighted on this page:

  1. Langmuir docs and staff: heights, THC, and machine limits.
  2. Razorweld data: the Razorcut 45 manual (a rough hand-cutting table, only two thicknesses), and this Razorweld-supplied chart (Razorweld to MO-Hacker). For machine settings I start from the chart and use the manual as a sanity check.
  3. The T45m-format chart reportedly supplied by Jasic (FSFab, t/45011). It’s a second candidate set, to be tested side by side in the test-cut program. Its Jasic origin is unconfirmed.
  4. Experienced forum members, then everyone else.

Caveats from the forum about the Razorweld chart:

  • PWCNC (t/847/30): the CrossFire only goes 300 ipm, and “I still make my own, test cut all material first”.
  • TinWhisperer (t/21266/8): a good start only; machines vary a lot unit to unit.
  • Another 847 poster says its speeds don’t suit the CrossFire.
  • The chart doesn’t name a torch, and it’s from 2019. It’s assumed here to fit the X45 machine torch; the test-cut program is meant to confirm or reject that.

Razorweld chart (my starting settings)
#

Source: Razorweld, via forum member MO-Hacker (t/847/29). Status: used by me, worked well, not formally tested.

Typed in from my saved image of the chart (view image) and checked cell by cell against the forum copy on 28 October 2026: every ipm, voltage, delay, and amps value matches. Use the Optimum column as the starting feed. “Max” is the fastest the chart says will still sever the metal, not a quality speed. ⚠ marks speeds over the PRO’s 300 ipm limit, which the PRO can’t reach. “—” means the chart gives no pierce delay.

The chart gives no cut height, pierce height, air pressure, or kerf. Use the defaults from Air supply, air quality, and X45 consumables, Pierce height, pierce delay, and cut height, and Kerf width and hole/part accuracy for those: 75 psi, pierce height 0.15 in, cut height 0.08 in in SheetCAM (0.060 in real gap), and kerf measured. The arc voltages are only valid at Razorweld’s unstated torch height (see Where sources disagree).

Oddities in the Razorweld chart (check these by test)
#

  1. Mild steel 14 ga max 640 ipm looks wrong. On every other row, Optimum is about 0.65 x Max (on stainless 18 ga it’s about 0.57). On mild 14 ga it’s 221 / 640 = 0.35. Also, 14 ga mild has a higher max than 18 ga mild (312), and stainless 14 ga’s max is exactly 221, the same number as mild 14 ga’s optimum. It may be a typing error in Razorweld’s original. Trust the 221 optimum only after a ladder test, and ignore the 640.

  2. The mm/min columns have typos that don’t match the ipm (ipm x 25.4):

    • stainless 22 ga optimum shows 6179 mm/min, but 322 ipm = 8179;
    • aluminum 3/32 optimum shows 4626, but 190 ipm = 4826;
    • smaller slips on mild 16 ga max, aluminum 1/16 max, 1/8 max, and 1/4 max.

    Use the ipm columns only.

  3. The 25 A rows have no pierce delay. It may mean no delay is needed on thin sheet, or just missing data. The test-cut program plans to test 0, 0.25, and 0.5 s.

  4. No cut height, pierce height, PSI, kerf, or consumable list. The chart can’t be copied whole. Those settings come from the other procedure pages (air, pierce height, kerf).

  5. The chart tops out at 40 A, though my cutter can do 45 A. Razorweld’s rule says full output on thick material (see Where sources disagree).

  6. Amps don’t follow thickness the same way in each material. Stainless jumps to 40 A at 18 ga; mild steel stays at 25 A through 16 ga. That’s why stainless 18 ga (335 ipm) is “faster” than mild 18 ga (203 ipm).

  7. Arc voltages are 139–152 V. That’s at or above the top of Langmuir’s “typical 60–150 V” (and the THC guide’s 90–150 V). It’s still inside the flowchart’s 85–230 V “in range” and well under the 280 V that can damage the THC. Arc voltage depends on torch height, and the chart doesn’t give one.

  8. Thin-sheet rows are mostly out of reach. Optimum speeds over 300 ipm on mild 26 and 22 ga, stainless 26, 22, and 18 ga, and aluminum 1/32 can’t be run on the PRO. Following Razorweld’s thin-material rule, the likely fix is lower amps at a reachable speed, found by test. The chart has no such rows.

T45m-format chart (FSFab reports Jasic supplied it; origin not confirmed)
#

⚠ Voltage conflict, read this first.

  • This chart says 111–123 V for 3/16 in and thinner, at a stated 0.06 in torch-to-work distance.
  • The Razorweld chart says 139–152 V, with no height given.

Row for row, they’re about 20–40 V apart. Arc voltage rises as the torch goes up (THC guide; Cletus), so either Razorweld’s numbers were taken higher off the plate or one chart is for a different torch. Don’t type either voltage into FireControl as fact. The test-cut program is planned to test both against SMART voltage, and measure the real torch height, before either is picked.

Where it comes from.

  • FSFab posted these charts in https://forum.langmuirsystems.com/t/45011 (2025-08-13). He says Jasic supplied them for the Razorcut 45 machine torch. Jasic builds the Razorcut.
  • He ran the 3/16 in settings on an XR with a Razorcut 45 machine torch. They cured a “pierces, then torch goes out” problem; he cut three test pieces start to finish.
  • Phillipw replied that they’re “very close to Hypertherm spec”.
  • Origin not confirmed. FSFab’s post says Jasic supplied this chart, but its layout, the “T45m shielded consumables” label, and the 400 ipm test-table footnote match Hypertherm’s Powermax45 charts. My saved copies (labeled Razorweld) are identical to the ones in FSFab’s thread.

How it’s used here.

  • It’s a second candidate setting set, no longer reference only. It’s weighted below the Razorweld chart (origin unconfirmed; one tester, a general forum member), and above other forum numbers.
  • The test-cut program runs its voltage as T2 in every session. There’s also an optional session comparing its 45 A, 3/16 mild steel settings against the Razorweld chart.
  • Amps differ: this chart uses 30 A and 45 A; the Razorweld chart uses 25 A and 40 A.

What it agrees on. Every row uses 0.06 in torch-to-work and 0.15 in initial pierce height (250 %). Both match Langmuir’s 0.060 in gap and 0.15 in pierce height (Pierce height, pierce delay, and cut height).

Chart notes:

  • Air flow 320 scfh hot / 360 scfh cold.
  • “*Maximum cut speed is limited by the test table’s maximum speed (400 ipm).” The PRO stops at 300 ipm.
  • “Edge start” means the chart recommends starting from the plate edge, not piercing.

What this does and doesn’t change
#

  • Voltage: see the warning at the top of this section. The test-cut program’s T1 uses the Razorweld chart’s voltage and T2 uses this chart’s voltage for the same thickness. For example:
    • 3/16 mild steel: 147 V vs. 118 V.
    • 16 ga mild steel: 152 V vs. 116 V at 45 A or 111 V at 30 A. The amps don’t match the Razorweld chart’s 25 A.
  • Pierce delays run short on Langmuir tables. Greg9504 (t/12941/2, also t/8193) quotes Langmuir staff: FireControl times the pierce delay from the torch-on command, not from arc-on. Book values like these assume arc-on, so add the time the arc takes to start. Treat this chart’s delays as a minimum. They’re already shorter than the Razorweld chart’s: 0.5 vs. 0.75 s on 3/16 mild, 0.1 vs. 0.25 s on 14 ga mild.
  • Speeds: a real alternative at 45 A, for example 3/16 mild at 85 ipm vs. the Razorweld chart’s 63 ipm at 40 A. Many thin rows are over 300 ipm and can’t be reached on the PRO.

Steps: dialing in one material and thickness
#

The full plan, with coupon, ladder, pierce test, THC test, and logging, is in the test-cut program. Short version:

  1. Fresh or known-good consumables, 75 psi at the gas check, clean dry air (Air supply, air quality, and X45 consumables). Clean flat scrap.
  2. Set the chart amps on the Razorcut knob (FireControl doesn’t set amps; Langmuir staff, thread 5883).
  3. Speed ladder around the chart optimum: −20 %, −10 %, 0, +10 %, +20 %. Capped at 300 ipm.
  4. Watch the arc under the plate (with eye protection; see Safety). Razorweld: the arc should leave the bottom “at a slight angle away from the direction of travel”.
    • Straight down means too slow, with dross build-up.
    • Sparks spraying up on top means too fast, or not enough amps.
  5. Grade dross, bevel, and top spatter. Langmuir: slow cuts leave heavy dross on the bottom; fast cuts leave small beads.
  6. Then test pierce delay, check the THC voltage, and measure the kerf on the winning setting (Pierce height, pierce delay, and cut height; THC setup, tuning, and stopping torch dives; Kerf width and hole/part accuracy).
  7. Log every coupon in the test-cut log. Put the winner in the results table below and in a SheetCAM tool with the amps in the tool name (SheetCAM 8.0 setup for FireControl).

Duty cycle
#

At 45 A, check the duty cycle against the Razorcut manual (25 %). Long 3/16 in jobs at 45 A can hit overheat errors E60/E61. Razorweld: leave the machine on so the fan cools it, and wait for the error to clear. Langmuir advertises 30 % at 45 A. Go with the manual’s 25 %.

Safety
#

Plasma arc light, fumes, and fire are covered by the Razorcut manual’s safety section and Langmuir’s safety page. Langmuir: never cut materials coated with paint, oil, grease, or solvents, and consult the MSDS. Razorweld: read the MSDS for the metal and any coating, and use ventilation or fume extraction. Galvanized or coated stock: follow those documents. This page doesn’t cover it. Use eye protection to ANSI Z87.1 / Z49.1 (Langmuir).

Fixed settings on the cutter
#

  1. Amps are set on the Razorcut knob. FireControl doesn’t control amps (Langmuir staff, thread 5883). Write the amps in the CAM tool name so you remember to set them.
  2. Air: 75 psi (Air supply, air quality, and X45 consumables).
  3. Trigger mode: check which mode your cutter is set to; 2T is assumed in the sources. The Razorcut manual describes 4T as latching and holding a pilot arc. Mesh mode keeps a pilot arc going for a while after the arc drops, and locks to 2T. Langmuir doesn’t say which mode to use with the CNC; 2T is the usual assumption because it simply follows the table’s on/off signal. Confirm with Langmuir (it’s on my list of support questions).
  4. Amps: start from the Razorweld chart (25 A on thin sheet, 40 A from 14 ga up). Razorweld’s general rule is: “On thick material, set the machine to full output and vary your travel speed. On thinner material… turn down the amperage.” That suggests 45 A for 3/16 in and 1/4 in, while the chart says 40 A. See Conflicts.

Where sources disagree (and the starting choice)
#

  • Razorweld chart vs. Razorcut manual vs. T45m/Jasic chart vs. forum. The Razorweld chart and the manual are both Razorweld data, and they mostly agree where they overlap. The chart is the more complete one for machine cutting, so ladders start from its Optimum column, with the manual used as a sanity check (and as the start for 3/16 aluminum). The T45m/Jasic chart is the second candidate (the test-cut program’s T2 and the optional 45 A session). Forum numbers are weighted last. All of it is unverified until the test-cut program’s results are logged.
  • 16 ga mild steel speed. Chart 114 vs. Cletus 150 vs. thread 847 72. A ±20 % ladder from 114 covers 91–137. If the best rung is at either end, run a second ladder beyond it.
  • 1/8 aluminum. Chart 133 vs. thread 847’s about 45. Too far apart to both be right for the same setup. Test from 133 and go down if needed. The 847 shorthand is unverified.
  • Amps on thick material. Chart 40 A vs. Razorweld’s “full output on thick material” (45 A). Starting choice: the chart’s 40 A. If 3/16 shows dross at every rung, try one ladder at 45 A.
  • Arc voltage. Chart 139–152 V vs. the community 115–128 V (thread 38627, an XR owner) vs. Langmuir’s “typical 60–150 V” and the Razorcut’s rated 98 V operating voltage. Don’t type chart voltages into FireControl as fact. SMART voltage stays the default (Langmuir staff, thread 5883). The test-cut program compares a fixed chart voltage to the SMART reading.
  • Arc voltage, T45m/Jasic vs. Razorweld chart (the big one). T45m: 111–123 V at 0.06 in (3/16 and under). Razorweld: 139–152 V at an unstated height. Both claim to be for the Razorcut 45. Planned 3-way test in the test-cut program: the SMART reading, the Razorweld chart’s voltage (T1), and the T45m chart’s voltage for that thickness (T2), with the real torch height measured.
  • Duty cycle: Razorcut manual (2022) 25 % vs. Langmuir 30 % at 45 A. Thread 236 quotes an older manual (30 % at 45 A, 20–40 A output). Go with the 2022 manual.
  • Jasic charts. Thread 45011 (FSFab): the T45m-format charts above, now a candidate in the test-cut program. Thread 44500: a search-result summary (not checked against the full thread) suggests those heights were for Jasic’s own CNC torch. Not used.
  • Pierce delays. Razorweld chart: 0.25–1.00 s on 40 A rows, none on 25 A rows. T45m/Jasic: 0.0–0.9 s, and it runs short on FireControl, which starts timing at the torch-on command (staff via Greg9504, t/12941/2). Starting choice: the Razorweld chart’s delays, then test them (the test-cut program).
